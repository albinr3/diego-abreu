# Module 10 — Paid Media, Creative Portfolios, and Conversion Windows

## Source and scope

This module is derived from a user-supplied transcript of a Diego Abreu podcast
appearance. It should be used as attributed framework material, not as a
technical specification of Meta's current delivery systems. Claims about
specific platform updates, including “Andromeda,” are volatile and must be
verified against current primary platform documentation when a user needs an
up-to-date implementation recommendation.

## Core distinction: conversion window, not attribution terminology

The key question in the transcript is how much persuasion a buyer needs before
the conversion event can reasonably happen. Call this the **conversion window**:
the time and evidence between first exposure and the action being optimized.

Do not confuse it with the reporting/attribution window. Attribution is how a
platform credits a result; the conversion window is the buyer's decision path.

### Short conversion window

For a simple, low-commitment action—for example, registering for a lead
magnet—the transcript argues that a long sequence of distinct messages may be
unnecessary before the opt-in. Evaluate whether one clear message and friction
reduction are more important than a complex multi-creative structure.

### Longer or higher-consideration conversion window

For services where booking requires more confidence, a buyer may need several
kinds of evidence before acting. The transcript's legal-services example uses
distinct creative roles:

- authority signals,
- the firm's story,
- case studies or proof,
- the mechanism or methodology,
- and explanations that reduce perceived risk.

In this situation, a complementary creative portfolio can make sense: its value
is not that every asset has the same job, but that the buyer can encounter the
reasons they need to book. This is particularly relevant to trust-sensitive,
regulated, complex, or higher-ticket services.

## Individual creative vs. portfolio evaluation

The transcript contrasts two lenses:

1. **Individual-creative lens:** Identify whether a specific message produces
   the desired conversion efficiently.
2. **Portfolio lens:** Evaluate whether a set of messages together supports the
   buyer's decision across a longer conversion window.

They are not mutually exclusive. Start with the conversion event and decision
path, then select the primary lens. A portfolio does not excuse weak individual
creative; an individually efficient creative does not prove that a complex
service needs no proof or authority elsewhere.

## Paid media as a multiplier

The transcript frames advertising as a controllable growth lever once the
commercial system works. Its usefulness comes from being able to compare spend
with downstream economics, not from clicks or ROAS in isolation.

Before increasing spend, establish:

- the conversion event being optimized,
- qualified-lead and show-up quality,
- close rate and collected revenue,
- CAC,
- gross profit per acquired client,
- delivery capacity and client results,
- and the maximum spend level at which economics remain acceptable.

“Front-end break-even” or immediate payback can be a powerful scaling position,
but it is not a requirement for profitable advertising. It should not hide cash
flow constraints, refunds, fulfillment cost, sales-team cost, or the risk that
extra volume exceeds delivery capacity.

## Organic and paid are complementary

The transcript rejects the false choice between organic content and paid ads:

- Organic content can establish authority and improve trust for prospects who
  arrive through ads.
- Paid media can distribute a proven message faster and more predictably.

The combined system should still be assessed by qualified customers, gross
profit, and delivery quality—not by the performance of either channel in
isolation.

## Practical diagnostic

Before recommending a Meta campaign structure, ask:

1. What exact action is the campaign optimizing for?
2. How quickly can a qualified prospect take that action after a first exposure?
3. What evidence must they see first: problem recognition, authority, proof,
   mechanism, risk reduction, or offer clarity?
4. Is the service trust-sensitive, complex, regulated, or high-consideration?
5. Which creative role is currently missing?
6. Which downstream metric determines whether the campaign works: qualified
   leads, attended calls, closed sales, CAC-to-gross-profit, or capacity?
7. At what spend point does marginal acquisition become unprofitable or exceed
   fulfillment capacity?
